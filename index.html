I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Southern Methodist University, on the 2026–2027 job market. I hold a master's degree in Economics from PIMES–UFPE and a bachelor's degree in Economics from UDESC.

I am an applied econometrician working on the economics of education and health, with a secondary interest in labor markets. My research uses quasi-experimental methods to study how early-life and school-based interventions shape children's long-run cognitive and socioemotional outcomes.

Job Market Paper

Mobile Internet and the Composition of Parental Time

I estimate the effect of mobile internet on parents' time with their children, exploiting the staggered rollout of 3G and 4G coverage across US metropolitan areas and time diaries from the American Time Use Survey, 2004–2016. Near-universal coverage raises total childcare by 42 minutes per day, about 10 percent, with effects emerging two years after coverage arrives. Parents do not cut leisure or home production to make this time; instead, home production is unchanged in total but increasingly done with a child present. Responses are similar for mothers and fathers. Suggestive evidence indicates that more-educated parents increase active care, while less-educated parents move leisure and household tasks into their children's presence.
